Persistence diagrams are precise but hard to read cold. The intuition behind them
is a *sweep*: pick a scale, connect everything closer than that scale, and watch the
shape of the data change as the scale grows.

## See it

Each point grows an "influence ball" of radius `scale / 2`. When two balls touch,
their points connect — and separate pieces merge (a **component**, H₀, dies). When a
ring of points is enclosed but not yet filled, there is a **loop** (H₁) alive; once
the interior fills with triangles, the loop dies. The counts below are the Betti
numbers `H₀` (components) and `H₁` (loops) at the current scale, from the same
reducer as `src/spatial/persistence.ts`.

Switch the **view** between the flat 2-D picture, a **cones** scale-space — each
point becomes a cone of its distance function, and a slice plane at the current
scale cuts out the 2-D disks, so sweeping the plane up the cones *is* the
filtration — or **both** side by side. Drag the cones to rotate.

## How to read it

- **`H₀` (components)** starts high — every point is its own island — and falls as
  balls merge islands together. The "two clusters" shape parks at `H₀ = 2` for a
  while before merging to `1`: that plateau is what makes two clusters a *real*,
  persistent feature rather than noise.
- **`H₁` (loops)** appears when a cycle of connections encloses empty space, and
  dies when that space fills in. The "ring" holds one loop across a wide band of
  scales; the "figure-eight" holds two. A feature that survives a wide band of
  scales is the robust topology — exactly what a [persistence diagram](/primitives/cknn/#try-it-the-persistence-diagram)
  records as a point far from the diagonal.

This is the **filtration** a Vietoris–Rips persistence reducer walks through. The
toolbox's job is to *build* this filtration efficiently — including the density-
adaptive [CkNN](/primitives/cknn/) variant — and hand it to the reducer, which reads
off how long each component and loop survives.

## Hard balls vs fuzzy fields

The sweep above grows **hard balls**: a point is in the set or it isn't, and the
boundary of a union of balls has *corners* where two balls meet. That hard threshold
is the same boxcar we replaced with a smooth window everywhere else in this toolbox
(see [Fuzzy TDA](/concepts/fuzzy-tda/)), and it has the same costs: the boundary is
non-smooth, and a single stray **outlier** spawns a full-strength ball at radius `0`
— a long, important-looking bar that is really just noise.

The **fuzzy** filtration replaces each hard ball with a smooth Gaussian kernel and
thresholds the resulting density field — the *superlevel-set filtration of a KDE*.
Now the boundary is C-infinity (no corners), and how strongly a region counts depends
on how much density is piled there, so sparse noise is demoted toward the diagonal.
The robust-statistics version, **distance-to-measure (DTM)**, makes this a theorem:
its persistence is provably stable to outliers in the bottleneck/Wasserstein sense,
which Čech/Vietoris–Rips is not.

Sweep the same parameter through both. Turn **outliers** on and watch the hard
diagram (left) sprout points far from the diagonal while the fuzzy diagram (right)
keeps the noise near it and the true feature prominent. Switch the fuzzy field
between **KDE** and **DTM**.

The second row of controls exposes the parameters that decide what counts as
structure. **Falloff** is the kernel bandwidth `σ` (or, for DTM, the neighbour count
`k`): widen it and nearby features merge; narrow it and a smooth ring shatters into a
necklace of separate density beads (`H₀` climbs). **Resolution** is the grid the field
is sampled on. **Noise band** is the persistence threshold below which a feature is
treated as noise — the shaded strip along the diagonal — i.e. the line between signal
and chatter that a hard filtration forces you to draw by hand.

Unlike the sweep above, this demo imports the toolbox's own library directly — the
fields come from [`src/spatial/scalarField.ts`](https://github.com/) (`gaussianKdeField`
— the CPU twin of the [KDE splat](/primitives/kde-splat/) primitive — `distanceField`,
`dtmField`) and the diagrams from `src/spatial/sublevelsetPersistence.ts`, a cubical
**sublevel-set** reducer (the field counterpart of the point-cloud Vietoris–Rips
reducer). Both the hard ball-union and the fuzzy field are reduced by that one
primitive: the only thing that changes is *which field you sublevel-set*.

When your browser exposes **WebGPU** (badge top-right), the fields are computed by a
compute shader — the same additive-Gaussian splat / brute-force gather the
[KDE splat](/primitives/kde-splat/) primitive uses — and the badge turns green; without
it, the CPU library runs instead (toggle it to compare — the diagram is identical). The
persistence reduction itself is inherently sequential and always stays on the CPU.
:::

  is [CkNN](/primitives/cknn/). The diagram conventions follow the
  [persistence diagram](/primitives/cknn/#try-it-the-persistence-diagram) primitive.

  [Bull. Amer. Math. Soc. 45 (2008)](https://www.ams.org/journals/bull/2008-45-01/S0273-0979-07-01191-3/)
  — the most readable entry point.

  [EPJ Data Science 6 (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjds/s13688-017-0109-5)
  — practical, computation-focused (open access).

  [SoCG 2019](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.SoCG.2019.58)
