Persistence diagrams are precise but hard to read cold. The intuition behind them
is a *sweep*: pick a scale, connect everything closer than that scale, and watch the
shape of the data change as the scale grows.

## See it

Each point grows an "influence ball" of radius `scale / 2`. When two balls touch,
their points connect — and separate pieces merge (a **component**, H₀, dies). When a
ring of points is enclosed but not yet filled, there is a **loop** (H₁) alive; once
the interior fills with triangles, the loop dies. The counts below are the Betti
numbers `H₀` (components) and `H₁` (loops) at the current scale, from the same
reducer as `src/spatial/persistence.ts`. Drag to rotate.

## How to read it

- **`H₀` (components)** starts high — every point is its own island — and falls as
  balls merge islands together. The "two clusters" shape parks at `H₀ = 2` for a
  while before merging to `1`: that plateau is what makes two clusters a *real*,
  persistent feature rather than noise.
- **`H₁` (loops)** appears when a cycle of connections encloses empty space, and
  dies when that space fills in. The "ring" holds one loop across a wide band of
  scales; the "figure-eight" holds two. A feature that survives a wide band of
  scales is the robust topology — exactly what a [persistence diagram](/primitives/cknn/#try-it-the-persistence-diagram)
  records as a point far from the diagonal.

This is the **filtration** a Vietoris–Rips persistence reducer walks through. The
toolbox's job is to *build* this filtration efficiently — including the density-
adaptive [CkNN](/primitives/cknn/) variant — and hand it to the reducer, which reads
off how long each component and loop survives.
