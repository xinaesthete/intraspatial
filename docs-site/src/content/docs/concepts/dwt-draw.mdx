The discrete wavelet transform is invertible, so the coefficient pyramid is not
a read-only picture — it is an *editable representation of the image*. This demo
lets you brush directly on the coefficients and runs the **inverse DWT** on every
stroke, so you can feel, rather than just read about, what each subband carries.

<DwtDraw client:only="react" />

## What to try

- **Erase a detail subband.** Pick the **Erase** brush and scrub over one of the
  detail quadrants (warm/cool speckle). Edges in the matching direction soften in
  the resynthesis — HL holds vertical edges, LH horizontal, HH diagonal — while
  the PSNR drops. This is manual, spatial compression: you are choosing which
  detail to throw away.
- **Inject a single coefficient.** Switch to **Add +** with a small brush and a
  high strength, then dab once in a coarse (large) detail band. A smooth,
  wavelet-shaped ripple blooms in the image — that one number *is* a basis
  function. Coarser levels paint broad ripples; finer levels paint tiny ones.
- **Erase all detail** to collapse the image to its LL approximation (a blurred
  thumbnail), then add detail back by hand.
- Toggle **edit LL too** to paint into the approximation band and watch large
  low-frequency blotches appear.

The `5/3` kernel is integer and reversible; `9/7` is the lossy floating-point
filter. With no edits, the inverse round-trips the original exactly (PSNR ∞).

:::note
This is the interactive sibling of the static [Wavelets &amp; the DWT
primer](/dwt-primer/), reimplemented as a React island so it can grow more
editing tools over time. The wavelet math is the project's own reference
implementation.
:::
