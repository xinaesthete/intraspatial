Co-location modes — the Gram matrix and its OKLab eigen-projection

Every channel is splatted through a unit-mass kernel and the whole K×K matrix is one product, C = MMᵀ. Channels come from either a categorical obs column or selected vars from X — a cell type is the one-hot case of a general per-cell mark, so both take the identical path. The map is the leading three eigenvectors of the standardised matrix, projected back onto the rasters and painted through OKLab: mode 1 to lightness, modes 2–3 to the two chroma axes, so perceived colour distance stands in for distance between co-location profiles. Derivation: docs/cell-stats.md §4.

channel × channel spatial correlation

PSD by construction (it is a Gram matrix of real vectors), diagonal exactly 1 — which is what makes the eigenvalues below genuine variance shares. Diverging over [−1, 1].

eigenvalue spectrum

λk/K — the fraction of total spatial variance in each mode. They sum to 1; a negative bar would mean the matrix was not PSD and the projection meaningless. 45% means nothing without a null — run one to find out how much of it is inevitable.

mode loadings

Rows = modes 1–3, bars = each channel's signed weight. Channels loading the same way co-vary in space; opposite signs mean spatial exclusion. Hover a column to read one gene across the modes.
